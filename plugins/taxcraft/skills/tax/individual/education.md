
# Education

Owns 529 plans, the §25A credits, and education-related deductions. The
§529-to-Roth **limits** live in `retirement.md` §4; this file records the
transfer decision.

All amounts and phaseouts → `authority.md`.

## 1. The coordination rule

**The same dollar of qualified expense cannot fund two benefits.** A dollar paid
by a tax-free 529 distribution, a tax-free scholarship, or an employer plan is not
available for AOTC or LLC.

⚠ **The scholarship-allocation move — and the two gates that decide it.** The
standard play is to make part of a scholarship **taxable to the student**, freeing
tuition for the parent's AOTC. Before recommending it:

1. **Scholarship terms.** Available only if the grant's terms permit application
   to non-qualified expenses (Prop. Reg. §1.25A-5(c)(3)). A scholarship
   **restricted to tuition cannot be reallocated at all**.
2. ⚠ **Kiddie tax — and the characterization is counterintuitive, so do not
   assume it.** `kiddie-dependents.md` §2 **owns** how a taxable scholarship is
   characterized for §1(g) and Form 8615; it is **not** restated here because the
   two regimes (the §1(g) *threshold* test and the Form 8615 *computation*) treat
   it differently. Load that file and apply its rule before concluding the play
   works or fails — then verify against the current Form 8615 instructions under
   `authority.md`, because this single characterization decides the whole
   strategy.

Then compare after-tax outcomes both ways.

## 2. AOTC vs. LLC

Both §25A; not both for the same student in the same year. AOTC: per student,
first four years, half-time, 40% refundable, no felony drug conviction. LLC: per
return, no year limit, no enrollment minimum, nonrefundable, covers graduate work.

⚠ Traps:

- **Form 1098-T is unreliable — but not for the usual reason.** Box 2 (amounts
  billed) was **eliminated**; institutions report **payments received** in box 1.
  The real problems: box 1 includes payments **from all sources, scholarships and
  529 distributions included**, so it overstates what the taxpayer paid; **box 7**
  flags a January–March term; **box 4** adjustments force prior-year recapture;
  and box 1 **excludes course materials**. What the credit needs is **adjusted
  qualified expenses** — amounts paid in the year, reduced by tax-free
  assistance. For a claimed dependent, payments by the **student** or a
  **qualifying third party** are treated as made by the claiming taxpayer
  (§25A(g)(3)), so "who wrote the check" is rarely the issue. Reconcile to the
  bursar's statement.
- **Course materials differ between the credits**: AOTC allows materials purchased
  **anywhere**; LLC requires materials paid **to the institution as a condition of
  enrollment** (§25A(f)(1)(A) vs. (D)).
- ⚠ **MFS cannot claim either credit** (§25A(g)(6)).
- A **December prepayment for a term beginning in the first three months** of the
  following year counts in the payment year.
- The credit **follows the dependency claim** (`credits.md`). ⚠ Before
  recommending that a phased-out parent **forgo the dependency** so the student
  claims it: ⚠ **§25A(b)(4)(B) bars the 40% refundable portion for a student who
  is subject to the kiddie tax** (broadly: under 18, or under 24 as a student with
  earned income at or below half of support, with a living parent and not filing
  MFJ) — usually the entire point of the move. Test the condition against
  `kiddie-dependents.md`; verify the subsection against the current form under
  `authority.md`.
- The AOTC has its own disallowance ban and **Form 8862** requirement under
  **§25A(b)(4)** — not §32(k), which is the EITC provision.
- ⚠ **TY2026 identity gate:** a work-valid SSN issued by the due date is required
  for the claimant and, where different, the student. Verify per year.

## 3. 529 plans

- **Ownership vs. beneficiary** — the owner controls; the beneficiary has no
  rights. ⚠ §529 assets are generally **excluded** from the owner's gross estate
  (§529(c)(4)(A)); the only inclusion is the pro-rata piece on death inside a
  five-year election. Grandparent-owned distributions no longer count as student
  income under the FAFSA Simplification Act.
- **Contributions are gifts.** ⚠ The **§529(c)(2)(B) five-year election**
  front-loads five annual exclusions, is reported on **Form 709 even with no tax
  due**, and a pro-rata portion returns to the estate if the donor dies inside the
  period. Form 709 is owned by `estate-gift.md`.
- **Qualified expenses** extend beyond college — K-12 tuition, apprenticeships,
  credential programs, and a lifetime student-loan cap. ⚠ OBBBA expanded several;
  verify scope and caps.
- ⚠ **Non-qualified distributions** — earnings are ordinary plus 10%, with the
  **penalty** (not the tax) waived for death, disability, scholarship,
  service-academy, and — importantly for §1 — **expenses used to claim a §25A
  credit** (§529(c)(6) → §530(d)(4)(B)). ⚠ **The waivers split into two kinds and
  the difference decides how much is actually relieved:** **death and disability
  are uncapped** — the penalty falls away on the whole distribution. The
  **tax-free-assistance, service-academy, and §25A-coordination** exceptions are
  **amount-limited**. ⚠ **The tax-free-assistance waiver is §530(d)(4)(B)(iii)**
  (not (ii), which is disability), **and it incorporates the whole §25A(g)(2)
  list, which is materially broader than scholarships** — it also reaches
  veterans' and Selected Reserve assistance allowances and other federally
  excludable education payments. **Read §25A(g)(2) from primary authority at
  point of use** under `authority.md`; do not truncate it to scholarships, and do
  not rely on a paraphrase of it here. ⚠ Note only that **excludability is the
  operative condition** — an amount that is *not* excludable from gross income is
  outside the category whatever it is called.
  The service-academy exception reaches the
  **cost of advanced education** at the academy; the §25A exception reaches the
  expenses actually used for the credit. A small scholarship does not exempt a
  large distribution. In every case the **earnings remain taxable**; only the 10%
  additional tax is waived.
- ⚠ **The 1099-Q goes to the recipient of the distribution.** Paid to the
  beneficiary or directly to the school it carries the **beneficiary's TIN**, and
  any taxable earnings land on the **student's** return, not the owner's. One of
  the most common real 529 errors.
- **Beneficiary changes** within the family are tax-free; a change to a **younger
  generation** is a GST transfer.
- ⚠ **State deduction recapture** — many states claw back a prior deduction on a
  non-qualified or rolled-out distribution. A no-income-tax state gives no
  deduction to lose.
- **§529-to-Roth** limits → `retirement.md` §4; record here only the transfer
  decision and the gating account facts (age, recent contributions, beneficiary).

## 4. Coverdell, bonds, loans, employer plans

- **Coverdell ESA** — lower cap, income-limited contributors, distributed by
  beneficiary age 30.
- ⚠ **§135 savings bonds** — the ownership rule kills most claims: the bond must
  be **issued in the name of the taxpayer, or taxpayer and spouse**, who must have
  been **24+ at issuance**. **A bond in the child's name never qualifies at any
  age.** MFS ineligible (§135(d)(3)); tuition and fees only, reduced by
  scholarships and 529 distributions; own MAGI (§135(b)(2)).
- ⚠ **Student loan interest (§221)** — only for a loan the taxpayer is **legally
  obligated** to pay, so a parent paying a loan in the child's name gets nothing.
  **The planning answer:** the payment is treated as a **gift to the student, who
  is then treated as having paid the interest** and may deduct it — provided the
  student is not claimed as a dependent that year.
- **§127 employer assistance** — ⚠ OBBBA **made the student-loan repayment
  component permanent** and indexed the cap beginning 2026; there is no sunset to
  verify.
- **§117(d)** qualified tuition reduction, and **Reg §1.162-5** work-related
  education as a Schedule C deduction — the only surviving path now that
  miscellaneous itemized deductions are suspended (`schedule-c.md`).
