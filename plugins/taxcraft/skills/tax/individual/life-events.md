
# Life Events

Owns events that change **filing status, basis, or eligibility** — not events
that merely add a number. Home-sale mechanics are `1040.md` §6; job loss is
`job-loss.md`; residency change is `state-residency.md`.

All amounts and effective dates → `authority.md`.

## 1. Marriage and filing status

The status-determination rule and its four statutory overrides are owned by
`1040.md` §1; record the one that applied in `wp-life-events.md`.

⚠ **MFJ↔MFS is a one-way door.** §6013(b) permits MFS → MFJ (with limits,
including a bar once a deficiency notice has been petitioned); **MFJ → MFS is not
permitted after the unextended due date.** Highest-consequence procedural fact
here.

MFS costs: compressed brackets, forced itemizing if the other spouse itemizes,
the §469(i) allowance to zero when living together, the §408A(c)(3)(B) Roth
phaseout collapsing to a near-zero band, the §1211(b) capital-loss allowance
halved, and general §36B PTC disqualification **except** under the
abuse/abandonment exception (Reg §1.36B-2(b)(2)(v)). It wins for
income-driven student loan repayment, a large medical or casualty deduction
concentrated in the lower-income spouse, or **§6013(d)(3) joint and several
liability** separation.

**Relief provisions — three distinct regimes:**

- ⚠ **§6015** innocent spouse: (b) and (c) carry a **two-year window from first
  collection activity**. **(f) equitable is not subject to that rule but is not
  open-ended** — under Rev. Proc. 2013-34 it must be requested **before the
  §6502 collection statute expires**, or **within the §6511 refund period** where
  a refund is sought. Form 8857.
- **§66(c)** — the separate provision for a **community-property** spouse where
  the item is the other spouse's community share. Legally distinct from §6015 and
  often the applicable path. → `state-residency.md` §7.
- **Form 8379 injured spouse** protects a refund from the other spouse's separate
  debt — not innocent-spouse relief. → `notices-amendments.md` §4.

## 2. Divorce

⚠ **Alimony: the execution date controls, permanently.** Post-2018 instruments
produce no deduction and no inclusion (TCJA §11051); pre-2019 keep the old
treatment **unless modified** to adopt the new rules — read the modification.
Pre-2019 alimony needs the recipient's SSN, is subject to recapture, and can be
recharacterized under **§71(c)(2)** where payments are child-contingent.
⚠ **Post-2018 alimony is not "compensation" for IRA purposes** (§219(f)(1)), so a
recipient cannot fund an IRA with it.

⚠ **§1041** transfers are nonrecognition with **carryover basis** — but **not
where the transferee spouse is a nonresident alien** (§1041(d)). "Incident to
divorce" uses the 1-year / 6-year presumption (Reg §1.1041-1T Q&A-7). The
consequence people miss: **$500K of cash and $500K of appreciated stock are not an
equal split** — model after-tax value, and route the resulting basis to its SSOT
(`1040.md` §5). §1041 also **carries an S-corp suspended loss to the transferee**
(§1366(d)(2)(B)).

⚠ **Retirement division** — **QDRO** for a plan, **§408(d)(6)** for an IRA;
neither is a taxable distribution. **The trap:** the §72(t)(2)(C) penalty-free
exception applies to a distribution **from the plan** to the alternate payee.
**Rolling it into an IRA destroys the exception permanently** — the same
structure as the age-55 forfeiture in `job-loss.md`, and often the only
penalty-free access that spouse has before 59½. A cash QDRO distribution carries
**20% mandatory withholding** (§3405(c)). → `retirement.md`.

**The residence** — §121(d)(3) attributes a spouse's **use** where the decree
grants occupancy, and **§121(d)(3)(A) tacks the transferor's ownership period** on
a §1041 transfer, which is what makes a post-divorce sale by the receiving spouse
work. Dependency and Form 8332 → `credits.md`.

⚠ **Update beneficiary designations** — the most common post-divorce omission,
and the designation controls over the decree (`estate-gift.md`, `records.md`).

## 3. Death

**Of a spouse:** joint return for the year of death (§6013(a)(3)); **qualifying
surviving spouse** for the **two following years** with a dependent child — the
step down from MFJ brackets is abrupt and worth modeling. Basis step-up and the
community-property rule → `estate-gift.md` §2; record the event and route each
basis change to its SSOT. **IRD has no step-up**; §691(c) is the offsetting
deduction. Portability → `estate-gift.md` §3. Inherited-account elections →
`retirement.md` §3.

⚠ **Carryforwards die with the decedent** — capital loss, NOL, and passive
carryovers do not pass to the estate or survivor. **§469(g)(2)** allows suspended
passive losses on the final return only to the extent they **exceed the step-up**.
Where a terminal year is foreseeable, using them first is real planning.

**Of any decedent:** final 1040 by the personal representative; **Form 56**
notifies the IRS of the fiduciary relationship. **Form 1310** claims a refund
unless a court-appointed representative files with the appointment — ⚠ and is
**not required where a surviving spouse files a joint return**. Post-death income
goes to Form 1041; a **§645 election** may treat a qualified revocable trust as
part of the estate. ⚠ **§213(c)**: medical expenses paid by the estate within one
year of death may be elected onto the decedent's **final 1040** rather than the
706 — material on a terminal-illness return.

## 4. Birth or adoption

A child born any time in the year counts for the full year. Trigger together: SSN
application (**required by the due date** for several credits → `credits.md`), the
**§72(t)(2)(H)** penalty-free distribution (mechanics → `retirement.md`),
dependent-care FSA and Form 2441, the adoption credit and its **timing rule**
(→ `credits.md`), 529 opening, and beneficiary updates.

## 5. Relocation and disability

⚠ Moving expenses are not deductible **except** for an active-duty member moving
under a military order incident to a PCS (**§217(g)**), with the reimbursement
exclusion under **§132(g)(2)**. The **§217(k)** suspension housing that carve-out
was scheduled to expire 12/31/2025 and was **made permanent by OBBBA**, which also
extended it to certain **intelligence community** members. Residency change →
`state-residency.md` §6. A retained-and-rented former residence carries the
**Reg §1.165-9(b)(2)** dual loss basis and the §121 clock (`1040.md` §6).

⚠ **§72(m)(7)** "total and permanent" is a harder standard than SSA disability and
is the one that governs §72(t). The **§86(e) lump-sum election** taxes Social
Security back-pay under **prior years'** rules without amending them — commonly
missed. ⚠ **ABLE (§529A)**: **SECURE 2.0 §124 raises the disability-onset age
from 26 to 46 for years beginning after 12/31/2025**, materially expanding
eligibility in TY2026; the **529-to-ABLE rollover**, ABLE-to-Work, and the §25B
credit for ABLE contributions were TCJA provisions sunsetting 12/31/2025 that
**OBBBA made permanent**. A power of attorney is **not** a Form 2848.

## 6. Debt cancellation and foreclosure

Routed here from `1040.md` §2 (1099-A and 1099-C). ⚠ **`wp-life-events.md` is the
sole home for both** — no second record elsewhere.

⚠ **A 1099-A is a disposition, not COD:**

- **Nonrecourse** — amount realized is the **full outstanding debt**
  (*Comm'r v. Tufts*, 461 U.S. 300 (1983)); entirely gain or loss, no COD.
- **Recourse** — **bifurcate**: gain or loss measured by FMV vs. adjusted basis
  (Reg §1.1001-2(a)(2)), **plus** COD of debt minus FMV under §61(a)(11).

So a foreclosure of a former residence produces a 4797/Schedule D event **and**
possibly ordinary COD, with the §121 path applying to the disposition half.

**§108 exclusions — the statutory ordering is §108(a)(2), and it is not the order
people assume:**

1. **§108(a)(1)(A) title 11** takes priority over everything (§108(a)(2)(A)).
2. ⚠ **§108(a)(1)(E)/§108(h) QPRI is applied *before* insolvency** unless the
   taxpayer **elects** to apply insolvency in lieu of it (**§108(a)(2)(C)**).
   Listing QPRI last inverts the default. But ⚠ **QPRI is NOT permanent** — it
   applies to a discharge before its statutory expiration **or one subject to an
   arrangement entered into and evidenced in writing before that date**, and was
   **not extended by OBBBA**. ⚠ **Test the written-arrangement branch before
   rejecting QPRI** — a workout agreed in writing before the cutoff qualifies even
   though the discharge itself falls after it. Verify availability for the target
   year; never present it as a matched pair with insolvency.
3. ⚠ **§108(a)(1)(B) insolvency** — permanent, but **§108(a)(3) caps the exclusion
   at the amount of insolvency immediately before the discharge**. A taxpayer
   insolvent by $1 excludes $1, not the whole discharge. Insolvency is measured
   by a worksheet counting **all** liabilities and **all** assets including
   exempt assets and retirement accounts, and that computation is where it is won
   or lost.
4. **§108(a)(1)(D) QRPBI** (elective) — insolvency does **not** apply to a
   discharge to which (D) applies (§108(a)(2)(B)). ⚠ **Test the §108(c)(3)
   predicate before the caps:** the debt must be **incurred or assumed in
   connection with real property used in a trade or business** and **secured by
   that property**, must be **qualified acquisition indebtedness** if incurred
   after 1992, and the exclusion is **unavailable to a C corporation**. Real
   property held for *investment* rather than in a trade or business does not
   qualify, and ⚠ **qualified farm indebtedness is carved out of QRPBI
   entirely** — it runs through §108(a)(1)(C) instead. ⚠ **Post-1992 does not
   mean "no refinancing":** §108(c)(4) treats debt from **refinancing** qualifying
   pre-1993 debt (or refinancing qualified acquisition indebtedness) as qualifying
   **to the extent it does not exceed the amount refinanced** — a
   commonly-refinanced pre-1993 mortgage is not disqualified by the refinance
   date. ⚠ **Two caps, both required:**
   **§108(c)(2)(A)** limits the exclusion to the excess of the outstanding
   principal over the FMV of the securing real property, and **§108(c)(2)(B)**
   further caps it at the aggregate adjusted bases of depreciable real property
   held before the discharge.

**§108(b) attribute reduction** is the price: NOLs, general business credits,
capital loss carryovers, **basis**, passive carryovers, and FTCs reduced in
statutory order on **Form 982** (a §108(b)(5) election reduces depreciable basis
first). ⚠ **§1017(b)(2) floors the basis reduction** — it may not exceed the
excess of aggregate bases over aggregate liabilities immediately after the
discharge. ⚠ **§1017(c)(1) additionally protects an individual's exempt
property** — property treated as exempt under title 11 takes no basis reduction
at all, which matters precisely in the bankruptcy cases where §108(a)(1)(A) did
the excluding. Route the basis change to its SSOT and the carryforward reductions to
`wp-carryforward-rollforward.md`.

⚠ **Not COD at all:** a purchase-price adjustment with the seller (§108(e)(5)); a
discharge that is really compensation or a gift; a debt disputed in good faith.
**A 1099-C is not conclusive** — issuance follows the creditor's identifiable-event
rules and the year on the form is frequently wrong. Student-loan discharges have
their own regime and dates; federal and state treatment diverge.

**Invariants:** every status change names its determining rule and date; alimony
records the **execution** date and any modification; a §1041 split records
after-tax value and routes basis to its SSOT; carryforwards lost at death are
enumerated before the final return closes; a 1099-A is analysed as a
**disposition** (bifurcated if recourse), never as COD alone; every §108 exclusion
has a worksheet or named statutory basis, with QPRI availability verified for the
year; beneficiary updates confirmed after marriage, divorce, and death; an
MFJ/MFS comparison is computed whenever MFS is plausible.

Verify with a licensed practitioner before filing. Divorce and estate matters are
legal work — coordinate with counsel before relying on any allocation.
