
# QSBS / §1202 Scenario

Trigger: client holds or is considering issuing/acquiring Qualified Small Business Stock (QSBS) under IRC §1202, is evaluating C-corp exit/exclusion planning, or asks about §1202/§1045.

This is the canonical home for QSBS/§1202 content. `entities/c-corp.md`, `scenarios/ccorp-tax-reduction.md`, `scenarios/equity-comp.md`, and `scenarios/k1-vc-pe.md` cross-reference here rather than repeating the rules. For a proposed or remedial issuance, load `scenarios/stock-issuance.md` first; it owns the closing sequence and per-tranche record.

For an LLC taxed as a C corporation, do not assume that an entity-law unit is
“stock” for §1202. Route even an existing-holding eligibility request through
`stock-issuance.md` and report the unit issue as
`UNVERIFIED — TAX-COUNSEL REVIEW` unless controlling authority resolves it.

**Test §1244 separately when relevant.** Section 1202 may exclude gain if the company succeeds; §1244 may provide ordinary loss if it fails. Cash/property stock can qualify for both, but services can support §1202 and cannot support §1244. Every issuance memo should show separate `ISSUANCE-DATE PRONGS SATISFIED — PROVISIONAL`, `ISSUANCE-DATE INELIGIBLE`, `UNVERIFIED`, or `NOT APPLICABLE` results rather than implying that the doctrines always travel together. Final qualification is tested at disposition or loss.

## Two Regimes by Acquisition Date

OBBBA (PL 119-21) amended §1202 for stock acquired after its 7/4/2025 effective date, while leaving prior law in place for stock acquired before that date. **Regime is fixed at acquisition — always establish the exact acquisition date first**, since it determines which set of rules applies to that specific tranche.

### Pre-7/5/2025 Stock (Prior Law)

- **Exclusion**: 100% of gain excluded once the stock is held > 5 years (for stock acquired after 9/27/2010; earlier-acquired tranches carry lower exclusion percentages and AMT-preference treatment — flag separately if encountered, they are rare at this point given the lookback).
- **Per-issuer cap**: greater of $10M (lifetime, per taxpayer per issuer) or 10× aggregate adjusted basis in the issuer's stock.
- **Issuer gross-asset test**: aggregate gross assets must be ≤ $50M at all times up to and immediately after the issuance of the stock in question.
- **§1045 rollover** available if sold before the 5-year hold is met (see mechanics below).
- **Separate-taxpayer planning** through gifts or nongrantor trusts may permit
  separately computed limitations, but only after the transfer, taxpayer,
  filing-status, trust, and sale facts pass the gates below.

### Post-7/4/2025 Stock (OBBBA, IRC §1202 as amended by PL 119-21)

- **Tiered exclusion by holding period** — no more all-or-nothing 5-year cliff:
  - 3-year hold: 50% exclusion
  - 4-year hold: 75% exclusion
  - 5-year hold: 100% exclusion
- **Issuer gross-asset test raised**: $75M (up from $50M), to be indexed for inflation after 2026.
- **Per-issuer exclusion cap raised**: greater of $15M (up from $10M, to be indexed for inflation after 2026) or 10× aggregate adjusted basis.
- **Partial-exclusion taxation** — the non-excluded portion of gain at the 50%/75% tiers is taxed in the 28%-rate gain group (the same bucket as collectibles/pre-OBBBA §1202 taxable portion), producing effective rates of roughly 14% (at the 50%-exclusion/3-year tier) and roughly 7% (at the 75%-exclusion/4-year tier) on the stock's total gain.
- **AMT preference**: under the new rules, the taxable (non-excluded) portion of gain is **not** an AMT preference item. This differs from old law, where 7% of the excluded gain on pre-9/28/2010-acquired stock was an AMT preference — note that nuance only if a pre-9/28/2010 tranche is actually in play, which will be rare for post-7/4/2025 stock by definition.
- **No regime-swapping** — stock cannot be converted from pre-OBBBA to post-OBBBA treatment via a §351 exchange, reorganization, or other transaction. The acquisition date of the original QSBS controls; a later exchange for "new" stock does not reset it into the post-OBBBA regime (and generally preserves the original acquisition date/holding period under the normal §1202/§351 continuity rules).
- **Guidance is thin** — as of mid-2026, IRS guidance interpreting the OBBBA §1202 changes (indexing mechanics, interaction with §1045, edge cases on the tiered-exclusion/28%-group mechanics) is still limited. Flag this as an area requiring ongoing monitoring before finalizing large-dollar planning around post-7/4/2025 stock.

## Qualification Checklist

All of the following must be satisfied for stock to be QSBS, regardless of which regime applies:

1. **C-corporation** — issuer must be a C-corp at issuance and (generally) through the holding period covered by the exclusion test.
2. **Original issuance** — stock acquired directly from the issuing corporation in exchange for money, other property (not stock), or services rendered to the corporation. Secondary-market purchases do not qualify.
3. **Qualified trade or business** — issuer must be conducting a "qualified trade or business" per §1202(e)(3). Excluded categories: health, law, accounting/consulting, financial services/brokerage, farming, natural-resource/mineral extraction, hospitality (hotels, restaurants), and other personal-service-type businesses listed in §1202(e)(3).
4. **Active business requirement** — at least 80% (by value) of the corporation's assets must be used in the active conduct of one or more qualified trades or businesses, tested throughout substantially all of the taxpayer's holding period.
5. **Gross-asset test** — measured before and immediately after issuance: ≤ $50M for pre-7/5/2025 stock, ≤ $75M for post-7/4/2025 stock (see above). Compute cash plus aggregate adjusted basis of other property, substitute FMV for contributed property as §1202(d)(2) requires, include issuance proceeds, aggregate predecessors, and apply the >50%-controlled-group rules.
6. **Redemption tests** — screen §1202(c)(3) and Reg. §1.1202-2. Test purchases from the taxpayer or related persons during the four-year period beginning two years before issuance, and significant issuer redemptions during the two-year period beginning one year before issuance. Quantify statutory/regulatory thresholds and exceptions, including the bona-fide termination-of-services rule; do not infer safety from the word “repurchase.” Monitor through the post-issuance window.

Document each prong contemporaneously at `entities/<slug>/corporate/qsbs-tracking/`: before/after balance sheets, contributed-property FMVs and adjusted bases, predecessor/controlled-group schedule, consideration and stock-basis schedule, cap table/tranche snapshot, redemption lookback and continuing monitor, qualified-business analysis, and active-asset schedule. Re-certify C-corporation status, qualified-business status, and the 80%-active-use test periodically throughout substantially all of the holding period. Working capital, R&E/start-up activities, subsidiaries, portfolio securities, and nonbusiness real estate require their specific §1202(e) rules rather than a generic “startup” label.

## §1045 Rollover Mechanics

- Available only after the taxpayer held the sold QSBS for **more than six months**. It is generally used to defer gain when stock is sold before the holding period needed for the desired §1202 exclusion.
- **60-day reinvestment window** — replacement QSBS must be purchased within 60 days of the sale. Do not impose a “different issuer” rule that does not appear in §1045; same-issuer original-issue stock must be tested on its own §1202 facts.
- **Basis carryover** — the taxpayer's basis in the replacement QSBS is the replacement's cost minus the deferred gain (i.e., built-in gain carries over); the holding period of the original QSBS tacks onto the replacement stock for purposes of the 5-year (or tiered, for post-OBBBA stock) test.
- Replacement stock must be purchased and held as investment property. A partnership may elect at entity level, and an eligible partner may make a partner-level election under Reg. §1.1045-1(c), subject to the regulation's interest, replacement-stock, allocation, and reporting limits. Model the entity- and partner-level routes separately; see `scenarios/k1-vc-pe.md`.

## Stacking / Multiplying the Exclusion

- A genuinely separate taxpayer may have a separately computed per-issuer
  limitation, but never count another cap from the label on a transfer alone.
  For spouses, apply §1202(b)(1) and (b)(3) to the actual filing posture: MFJ
  uses one joint-return limitation, and excluded gain used on a joint return is
  allocated equally between spouses for later-year limitation computations;
  the dollar cap is halved for a married individual filing separately. A gift
  to a spouse therefore does **not** automatically double the couple's
  exclusion ceiling.
- **Gifting QSBS** to children or funding a nongrantor trust can raise a
  separate-taxpayer question, not a promised extra exclusion. Verify grantor-
  trust status, §643(f), gift/GST consequences, state fiduciary taxation,
  assignment-of-income and prearranged-sale facts, and each recipient's own
  disposition before counting any separate limitation. Obtain written tax and
  estate-counsel confirmation for stacking plans.
- The gift/contribution is not itself a taxable disposition of QSBS for exclusion purposes; the transferee's holding period tacks onto the transferor's (carryover basis and holding period).
- This is genuinely load-bearing trust and gift-tax planning — funding non-grantor trusts implicates grantor-trust rules, gift-tax use of exemption/GST allocation, and state fiduciary-income-tax siting (e.g., Nevada/Delaware/South Dakota non-grantor trusts for state-tax-free treatment). **Engage estate/trust counsel before executing** — this skill produces workpapers and issue-spotting, not trust instruments; see `entities/README.md` (Non-Goals: do not draft engagement letters or represent the taxpayer; the same boundary applies to trust documents).
